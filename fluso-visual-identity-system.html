Visual identity system

Human warmth, private structured intelligence, and modular clarity. The system combines quiet Swiss editorial structure with organic light, tactile image texture, and a dot language that can scale from logo to icon to atmosphere.

Texture directions

01

Core principles

Build from contrast. The brand should feel calm at rest and intelligent in detail: generous white space, exact typography, natural photographic warmth, and modular dot systems used with intent.

Tone

Quiet confidence

Lead with restraint. Let space, typography, and precise hierarchy do the work before adding decoration.

Use black, white, and soft grey as the default interface language.
Use color to define product zones and page moods, not every component.

Texture

Tactile intelligence

Dither, dot fields, and painterly textures make the digital system feel human without becoming nostalgic.

Layer dots over natural imagery when the page needs depth.
Keep texture secondary to content and interaction.

Structure

Editorial grid

The source slides use disciplined margins, a slim rail, large type, and almost no ornament. Preserve that grammar on the web.

Favor wide bands, strong alignment, and clear section rhythm.
Avoid nested cards and over-rounded SaaS surfaces.

System

Modular dots

The dot is the smallest unit. It becomes the mark, the icon system, image treatment, dither pattern, and motion vocabulary.

Icons should be built on 3x3, 4x4, or 7x7 dot grids.
Dots may vary in size, opacity, or density, but never become random noise.

The foundation is monochrome. Accent families come from the supplied product slides and painterly texture references. Use them as environments and product identifiers, not as a rainbow UI kit.

05

Imagery and texture

Natural imagery grounds the brand in warmth. Dither and dot treatments bridge the human and AI worlds, blending organic light with structured intelligence.

Basic imagery

Raw natural forms

Use mountains, trees, skies, rooms, materials, and people as the foundation. Keep them calm, credible, and spacious.

Executed light

Soft color and subtle dots

Light pages use washed color, quiet contrast, and dot overlays that nearly disappear into the canvas.

Executed dark

Deeper atmosphere

Dark moments should feel immersive and cinematic, but still structured by visible dot fields and clean geometry.

Composition rules

Hero headline should be literal and direct. Avoid abstract slogans that hide the product.
Use a real bitmap texture, natural image, or product state as the hero atmosphere. Avoid generic gradients.
Keep hero text unboxed. Do not put the primary message inside a floating card.
Create depth with dither masks, dot fields, and image contrast instead of glow effects.
Use one dominant accent family per page. Let other accents remain in tiny metadata or product switches.

Text states swap

Micro confirmation

Use text swaps for copy, saved, synced, and completed states. The action stays in place while the label changes.

Notification badge

Small attention cue

Use badge motion for rare alerts, unread states, and product signals. The diagonal pop should be quick and earned.

Panel reveal

Contextual detail

Use panel reveals for filters, side details, and short explanations. The surface appears from its trigger.

Number pop-in

Metric emphasis

Use for stats, pricing, usage, and performance. Keep digits tabular so the layout never jumps.

Icon swap

Stateful tools

Use for toggles, mode changes, and completed actions. Keep both icons in one stable square.

Motion rules

Animate state, not decoration. Each movement should explain what changed.
Default to opacity and transform. Avoid large layout, blur, or background animations.
Use dot motion where it reinforces the Prem mark: loaders, reveals, atmospheric grids, and product transitions.
Keep all motion optional through `prefers-reduced-motion` and make static states look finished.
09

Usage rules

Consistency matters more than decoration. These rules keep the identity recognizable across landing pages, docs, product surfaces, and campaigns.

Do

  • Use black headline openings with grey continuation text for brand statements.
  • Use product colors as systems: sage for Studio, clay for Chat, blue-grey for API.
  • Build icons from modular dot grids and keep them visually related to the symbol.
  • Combine natural imagery with dither or dot overlays when a page needs emotional warmth.
  • Keep surfaces rectangular or subtly rounded, with 4px to 8px radius for most UI.
  • Leave generous margins and let large type create hierarchy.

Do not

  • Do not turn the texture palette into a multicolor gradient system.
  • Do not use dots as random confetti. They should imply structure, image, or function.
  • Do not overuse cards inside cards or heavy dashboard chrome on brand pages.
  • Do not tint every icon. Product icons may be colored, topic icons should usually be monochrome.
  • Do not crop imagery so tightly that the natural setting becomes unreadable.
  • Do not use artificial glow, blur blobs, or decorative orbs as a substitute for image atmosphere.
